Unconstrained · Gabriel vowel clusters only · Gabriel at both stages

Every village has nine categorical cells, one independently fitted cluster label for each vowel. At 9/9, a profile is exactly one distinct row. At relaxed thresholds, a group must share the same named vowel cells across every member. No geographic constraint is applied at either stage. Colours and group numbers are local to the selected analysis and threshold.

Threshold hierarchy and stability

Bootstrap ARI compares the entire partition (1 = same, 0 ≈ chance); Jaccard compares each village’s group membership. Exact-row return requires all nine label-matched cells to recur. Tie-order ARI measures sensitivity to equally scored greedy merges. These are descriptive checks, not evidence that a dialect boundary exists.

Groups and their shared cores

For relaxed groups, “—” means that vowel’s cell varies within the group. The shared core is the columns that agree across all members. Within-group pair retention is the fraction of speaker-bootstrap replicates where original group members still group together, conditional on joint presence.

Calculation and cautions

All methods use the same complete nine-vowel village sample. Anchors are coordinate-wise medians of speaker token medians; Gotland and Finland are eligible. Each vowel gets its own Ward clustering, with prescribed cuts at 2, 3 or 4 groups. Position uses two coordinates at 50%; trajectory uses six at 20/50/80%; movement uses four differences (50−20 and 80−50, each with two coordinates), removing absolute position. One pooled SD per acoustic axis within vowel and feature-set balances the two axes. Scales and spectral PCA remain fixed during bootstrap.

Exact profiles are literal row equality, unaffected by any within-column relabelling. For relaxed levels we progressively merge groups only when their union retains at least the requested number of common cells. Priority is the strongest shared core, then larger combined membership, then deterministic alphabetical/internal ordering. Groups are nested from 9 to 3. This is stronger than “every pair matches on some m vowels”: those pairs might not share the same m vowels across the group. It also avoids connected-component chains. But the greedy partition is not unique or globally optimal; equally eligible merges can lead to different groups. The tie-order audit randomizes only that final tie resolution.

Whole speakers are resampled jointly across vowels and timepoints. Villages missing any resampled anchor are omitted only for that replicate, without imputation. All nine vowel partitions and the profile hierarchy are refitted. Exact-row return uses optimal per-vowel label alignment; group ARI, Jaccard and pair retention do not depend on label numbers. Coverage counts are exported. Uncertainty is conditional on retained villages and excludes token-level, tracking, lexical and PCA-training uncertainty.

Prescribing several clusters per vowel forces categorical distinctions even where variation is continuous. Nine-cell equality compounds individual cell uncertainty; more exact profiles or cleaner-looking map groups need not mean better dialect regions. Low k can produce common rows simply because some vowel columns have one dominant cluster. Check input diagnostics, sensitivity to k, and common-core membership rather than selecting the most attractive map.

Singleton caution: partition ARI and membership Jaccard can look high when both partitions mostly contain singletons. That does not mean the nine-cell patterns themselves are stable, nor identify shared regional profiles. Read singleton counts alongside exact-row return and non-singleton pair retention; singleton groups have no pair-retention score.

Geographic coherence is partly built in whenever a geographic constraint is used; it is not independent validation.

Gabriel versions use the full coordinate-only graph, without the 100-unit cutoff of the older local-comparison viewer. The implemented criterion is an empty closed diameter disk, with a small numerical tolerance; no graph edges are silently added. Long links are possible in sparsely sampled areas, including across water. Ward chooses the smallest acoustic SSE increase among adjacent groups. Each cluster is connected, but adjacency imposes no smoothness penalty. Speaker bootstraps rebuild the graph on the retained villages when resampled vowel coverage removes villages. This retains the speaker bootstrap of our preceding analysis, not the village-resampling scheme used in the linked contrast report.
