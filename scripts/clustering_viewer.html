Exploratory clustering of complete nine-vowel village configurations

These are acoustic similarity groups, not assumed dialect regions. Geography is overlaid only after clustering. Choose 2–6 groups and compare methods and timepoints. Cluster numbers and colours are arbitrary and need not identify the same group across analyses.

Initial reading

These results support inspecting recurring local similarities, but not treating one partition as an established map of dialect regions. Adding all timepoints changes the partition; it does not consistently make clustering more stable. Compare the co-clustering blocks and cluster means, not just the map colours.

Partition diagnostics

Silhouette describes acoustic separation (higher is better separated; near zero suggests overlapping groups). Bootstrap adjusted Rand index (ARI) compares each resampled partition with the original on retained villages: 1 = identical, 0 ≈ chance-level agreement. Its 2.5–97.5% range is a descriptive resampling range, not a hypothesis test.

Grey acoustic points show individual village anchors; coloured/labeled anchors are arithmetic means across the villages in each cluster, with equal village weights. Arrows show the six original vowel pairs; /iː/ remains unpaired. No geographic smoothing or Procrustes alignment is used.

Ward dendrogram

Ward linkage merges groups to minimize the increase in within-group squared Euclidean variation.

Method and limitations

We first calculate coordinate-wise medians of tokens for each speaker, vowel and temporal point, then coordinate-wise medians across speakers to obtain village anchors. Only villages with all nine vowels and both coordinates at all three timepoints in both methods are included, giving the same village sample in all four analyses. Incomplete villages are listed in the exclusions CSV. Gotland and Finland are eligible.

Each configuration concatenates the two coordinates of all nine vowel anchors: 18 features for 50% alone, 54 for all three timepoints. Each acoustic axis is scaled by one pooled SD across the included village anchors, vowels and selected timepoints. No individual vowel or village is separately normalized. The concatenation is divided by the square root of the number of timepoints, keeping distance units comparable. Translation, size, orientation and relative vowel positions are all retained. Ward hierarchical clustering is fitted independently for each method and timepoint set; cuts give exactly 2–6 groups. Scaling is fixed during bootstrapping.

Whole speakers are resampled with replacement within each village. Their available vowel trajectories are kept together, village medians are recalculated, and clustering is repeated. If any anchor is missing after resampling, that village is omitted only for that replicate. Co-clustering uses only jointly retained replicates. ARI uses the retained subset of the original partition. Village membership agreement uses optimal label matching within each replicate and is reported alongside each village’s retention count. All stability estimates are conditional on resampled coverage and are limited by the small speaker samples. Tokens are not resampled and the spectral PCA is not refitted.

Ward clustering always produces groups, even when variation is gradual. Inspect small clusters and outliers rather than treating a high silhouette as proof of regions. All-timepoint clustering combines static position and dynamics; it does not isolate diphthongization. The two timepoint analyses overlap heavily and are not independent confirmation. PCA and formants describe different acoustic information, so agreement is informative but disagreement is not automatically an error.
